## 2. Blunt Defenses for the Five Toughest Jury Questions

### Question 1: "What if your anaemia or skin classifier fails on rural UP children?"
> **Blunt Defense:** *"That is exactly why we refuse to use naive smartphone cameras as diagnostic tools. In rural Bihar (Communications Medicine, 2023), Western nailbed anaemia models drifted by ±4.43 g/dL due to henna, dirt, and lighting. That is why Bal-Setu uses **haemoglobin meter LCD OCR as our primary anaemia rail**, confines CalibCard multi-region conjunctival pallor to secondary pre-screening, and enforces an **Internal Amber tele-verification gate** where a Block Medical Officer reviews uncertain cases before a daily-wage family travels 40 km to a DEIC. Furthermore, Stage 1 of our pilot runs an IEC-approved 2,500-child shadow trial at KGMU against gold-standard laboratory CBC before routine deployment."*

### Question 2: "ASHAs already complain about e-Kavach and Poshan Tracker—why will they use another app?"
> **Blunt Defense:** *"They will not—and Bal-Setu never asks them to. Bal-Setu is engineered as an **offline Android Intent SDK / AAR plugin** invoked directly from inside e-Kavach. The ASHA enters zero duplicate household data: she taps 'Screen Child' in e-Kavach, Bal-Setu completes the 90-second sweep, returns structured FHIR observations, and automatically verifies her ASHA incentive upon hospital QR scan. It saves her 10 minutes per child compared to paper registers."*

### Question 3: "How do you comply with DPDP Section 9 when capturing children's photos and voices?"
> **Blunt Defense:** *"By keeping raw media off the cloud entirely. Under our **Zero-Raw-Media Edge architecture**, on-device LiteRT and OpenCV extract non-reconstructible numerical metrics (Hirschberg displacement, Hausdorff score, LCD digits) and immediately wipe raw images and audio from local memory. Only structured FHIR observations sync. For verifiable parental consent under Rule 10(1)(a), we reuse the mother's KYC-verified e-Kavach guardian ID, staying strictly within Fourth Schedule Part A healthcare delivery boundaries and hard-locking `DPDP_S9_3_LOCK = true` against all commercial tracking."*

### Question 4: "Won't WhatsApp messaging bankrupt the health budget, and what about mothers without smartphones?"
> **Blunt Defense:** *"Under Meta's October 1, 2026 pricing, a daily WhatsApp bot would indeed cost ₹19.45 Crore/year in UP. We do not run daily bots. We send **one single ₹0.1357 weekly digest** linking to a zero-fee Offline Mini-PWA (<2 MB) cached in the phone's browser, dropping WhatsApp spend by 96.6% to just ₹66 Lakh/year statewide. For the ~35% of rural UP homes without WhatsApp, we provide **toll-free missed-call Bhashini dialect IVR** in Hindi, Bhojpuri, Awadhi, Bundeli, and Braj, delivering 90-second voice play coaching with simple keypad confirmation."*

### Question 5: "Your AI is not CDSCO-licensed yet—how can the Health Department pilot it?"
> **Blunt Defense:** *"Under CDSCO's July 2026 final MDSW guidance, standalone AI triage software is regulated as **Class B SaMD** under the State Licensing Authority. Phase 1 therefore deploys legally under an **Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) clearance and an NSWS Form MD-12 Test Licence** at KGMU and JNMC Aligarh. This silent shadow evaluation generates the Indian pediatric clinical evidence required to submit the full Form MD-3 dossier alongside an Algorithm Change Protocol (ACP) for post-market updates."*
